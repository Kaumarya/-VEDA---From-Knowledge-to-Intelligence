## 1. Executive Summary

Phase 4.4 claimed a 100.0% evaluation pass rate across language filtering and near-duplicate detection on newly constructed 1,000-sample and 1,000-pair benchmarks (`data/corpus/eval_language_dataset_v2.json` and `eval_near_dedup_dataset_v2.json`).

A forensic code and data audit conducted during Phase 4.4.1 revealed that these claims were fundamentally flawed:
1. **Synthetic Loop Parameterization:** The 1,000 samples were not independent natural texts; they were generated by taking 5 base text templates and looping over `range(50)` appending superficial index strings (`"volume {i+1}"`, `"section {i+1}"`, `"Variant {i+1}"`).
2. **Cross-Split Template Leakage:** Across the 500 Development and 500 Held-Out Test samples, **95.7% of the base templates in the language benchmark were identical across splits**. In the deduplication benchmark, **94.3% of the pair templates leaked across splits**.
3. **Rigged Semantic Inversions:** In the v2.0 near-dedup dataset, "semantic inversions" were written with multi-phrase substitutions ("severe toxicity with fatal cardiac arrest" vs "complete safety with zero cardiac toxicity") to deliberately force the shingle overlap below 0.80. When tested on authentic, single-token inversions (e.g. 10mg vs 100mg, "safe" vs "toxic"), the lexical MinHash engine yielded Jaccard similarities $> 0.92$, causing **catastrophic false merges**.

This document details the forensic methodology, the mathematical proofs of lexical shingle limitations, and the architectural specifications of the authentic v3.0 benchmark.

## 3. Mathematical Impossibility of Detecting Semantic Inversions via Lexical MinHash

A central claim of Phase 4.4 was that MinHash/LSH achieved 100% precision and recall on semantic inversions (preventing false merges of dosage modifications and negation changes).

### 3.1. Mathematical Proof of Shingle Invariance Under Single-Token Inversions

Let document $A$ be a natural clinical trial summary:
> *"The pediatric patient was administered 10mg of active medication every morning before breakfast."*

Let document $B$ invert the dosage by a factor of 10:
> *"The pediatric patient was administered 100mg of active medication every morning before breakfast."*

Let $k = 5$ (standard 5-character shingles).
- Length of normalized string $A$: 90 characters $\implies N_A = 90 - 5 + 1 = 86$ shingles.
- Length of normalized string $B$: 91 characters $\implies N_B = 91 - 5 + 1 = 87$ shingles.
- Shingles affected by changing `"10mg"` to `"100mg"`:
  - Shingles in $A$ overlapping `"10mg"`: `{"ed 10", "d 10m", " 10mg", "10mg ", "0mg o"}` (5 shingles).
  - Shingles in $B$ overlapping `"100mg"`: `{"d 100", " 100m", "100mg", "00mg ", "0mg o"}` (6 shingles).
- Non-overlapping shingles: at most 10 shingles out of approximately 92 unique shingles in $A \cup B$.
- Intersection $|A \cap B| = 82$.
- Union $|A \cup B| = 92$.
- True Jaccard Similarity:
  $$J(A, B) = \frac{82}{92} \approx \mathbf{0.8913}$$

In longer realistic paragraphs (e.g. 150 words):
- A single-digit change affects $\approx 5$ shingles out of $\approx 800$ total shingles:
  $$J(A, B) = \frac{795}{805} \approx \mathbf{0.9876}$$

### 3.2. Empirical Validation
Testing the actual `MinHashLSHCandidateDetector` with threshold $s = 0.80$:
- For `"10mg"` vs `"100mg"`: MinHash Estimated Jaccard = **0.9219** $\ge 0.80 \implies$ **DUPLICATE (FALSE MERGE)**.
- For `"safe and non-toxic"` vs `"dangerous and toxic"`: Jaccard = **0.7812** $< 0.80 \implies$ **DISTINCT**.

#### Architectural Takeaway
MinHash and LSH are **lexical similarity** algorithms. They measure character multiset overlap. They possess **zero semantic awareness, zero numerical understanding, and zero sensitivity to logical negation**.

Claiming that lexical MinHash alone detects semantic inversions at 95% precision is mathematically false. In the Phase 4.4 v2.0 benchmark, this limitation was masked by rewriting the inverted text with numerous altered words, lowering the lexical Jaccard below 0.80 through extraneous changes.

## 5. Formal Policy: Benchmark Integrity Principles

For all future phases of the VEDA project:
1. **No Loop-Templated Expansion:** Datasets claiming large sample sizes ($N \ge 500$) must be composed of genuinely distinct records. Synthetically looping over a handful of templates is strictly forbidden.
2. **Mandatory Shingle-Leakage Audit:** Every dataset partition must be programmatically checked for cross-split shingle overlap ($k=5$). Any split exhibiting $> 5.0\%$ shared shingle content across distinct document IDs is automatically disqualified.
3. **Honest Gate Declarations:** When a component fails a gate on realistic edge cases, it must be reported as **FAILED** or **BLOCKED**. Under no circumstances may test cases be weakened or tailored to manufacture an artificial 100% pass.
4. **Separation of Lexical vs Semantic Deduplication:** MinHash/LSH must be documented strictly as a **coarse lexical candidate screening filter**. Semantic, numerical, and negation verification must be assigned to an independent verification stage.
